Leaving the roof its natural black and popping those pre-painted silver vents right onto it will look incredibly sharp and save you a ton of masking time.

You are spot-on that the painting will be the real investment of time for the depot. Wood absorbs paint very differently than plastic or resin, which makes your choice of approach a lot of fun:
* To Keep the Wood Grain: If you want it to look like stained, weathered lumber, using thin acrylic washes or alcohol-based stains lets that natural wood texture peek through beautifully.
* For a Solid Painted Look: If you are going for a standard railroad company paint scheme (like a traditional two-tone gray, cream and red, or yellow), a quick primer coat is your best friend. It seals the wood so it doesn't just drink up your Micro-Mark or craft paints, giving you a smooth, even finish.

Since the construction is pretty straightforward, it should be a much more relaxing pace than the bank marathon. Are you thinking of painting the depot pieces while they are still flat in the laser-cut sheets, or do you prefer to build the shell first and paint it assembled?98

Those plastic or resin detail sprues are standard for N-Scale Architect kits, and having the windows start out gray gives you a perfectly neutral base coat to build on.

If you decide to deviate from the standard olive green and throw a deep burgundy or a rich brown onto those privy window frames, it will look incredibly sharp. Here is how those two color choices play out for a railroad structure:

* The Deep Burgundy Route: This would be a fantastic design choice. Using a rich burgundy on the small privy windows subtly ties the entire station area to the SRRX burgundy on your Gorge-ous Grub diner right next to it. It makes the whole scene feel like a cohesive, intentional part of the Silver Ridge Railroad layout rather than just random kits dropped together.

* The Rich Brown Route: Going with the brown keeps the privy uniform, utilitarian, and low-profile. It blends the windows seamlessly into the rest of the structure's callouts, letting the main depot and the green Overton coach take center stage.

Since those tiny windows are on a sprue, you have the ultimate painter's advantage. You can easily airbrush the entire sprue in one quick pass or hand-paint them comfortably while the sprue gives you a solid handle to hold onto.
