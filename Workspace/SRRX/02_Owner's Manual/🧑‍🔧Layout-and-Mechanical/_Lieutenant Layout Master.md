# SRRX MASTER INFRASTRUCTURE & TRACK DATA (V10-FULL)
# Authority: Lieutenant Layout

## 1. PHYSICAL DIMENSIONS & SUBSTRATES
* **Total Benchwork Footprint:** 35-square-foot open grid framework supported by L-girders.
* **The Substrate Rule:** Every physical calculation must strictly discriminate between the two main legs due to different substrate profiles.
    * **North Leg Stack:** 1" pink extruded foam glued to 11/32" plywood subroadbed. Total structural thickness = 1-5/32".
    * **East Leg Stack:** 2" extruded foam glued to 11/32" plywood subroadbed. Total structural thickness = 2-11/32".
* **Roadbed:** 1/8" Atlas cork roadbed used on the main line. Ballast will cover cork, except in the yard, where landscape will be built up so that effectively the track will sit on the ground level as if there is no cork roadbed. Alternatively, there will be no cork laid in the yard, but this has not been determined.

## 2. STRUCTURAL FRAMING OBSTRUCTIONS
* **L-Girder Webs:** Translucent gray lower webs and yellow top flanges run beneath the main layout spans.
* **Keeper Joists (Golden Yellow):** 1x3 lumber profiles (actual vertical depth 2.75") mounted flush against the underside of the 11/32" plywood.
* **Leg Blocks (Red-Brown):** Solid laminated 2x4 lumber blocks creating an actual rigid footprint of 3.0" x 3.5" directly supporting the plywood.

## 3. TRACK & TURNOUT SPECIFICATIONS
* **Scale:** N-Scale (1:160).
* **Track Ecosystem:** Atlas sectional and flex track exclusively for all mains, sidings, and spurs.
* **Main Line/Siding Turnouts:** Atlas N-scale turnouts.
* **Yard Ladder Turnouts Only:** Peco N-scale turnouts exclusively.
* **Western Throat Geometry:** Yard ladder track paths run on a sharp Northwest-to-Southeast diagonal. Turnout throwbars require a diagonal Southwest-to-Northeast mechanical stroke.

## 4. DESIGNATED CHOKE-POINT RESOLUTIONS
* **Turnout #1 (Main-to-Yard Split):** Point throwbar sits in completely open air to the left of the keeper joist. Uses standard under-benchwork vertical Tortoise mounting.
* **Turnout #2 (Yard Track #1 Split):** Point throwbar sits in open air to the left of the keeper joist. Downstream diverting rails cross over the joist. Uses standard under-benchwork vertical Tortoise mounting.
* **Turnout #3 (Yard Track #2/3 Split):** Point throwbar now clear of the leg assembly.
* **Tortoise Switch Machines:** Tortoises will be used to switch most of the turnouts, and all of the yard. The yard Peco turnouts are of a vintage that they do not need frog power. The atlas turouts are a variety of power routing and insul-frog types. There are currently no electro-frog turnouts.

## 6. ELEVATED TRACK
* **Mine and Smelter Run Concerns:** The mine spur and the smelter spur have dramatic grades on them, with the smelter spur being nearly 4%. The challenge with the mine spur is the planned switchbacks to achieve the elevations.
* **The Mine Run:** The mine spur begins in the yard and goes around the western loop to come around to the southern side of the north leg and cross over the mainline just near the foot of the mountain. This is achieved through a combination of built up solid risers made from foam, and open piers and trestles (all yet to be identified and located). The grades are designed to be gentle (<3%) as it switches back across the face of the mountain to the mine. The operations aspect are covered in the Operator's Handbook.
* **The Smelter Spur:** The smelter spur is a turnout off the mainline at the southern side of the north leg. It is a relatively straight track that climbs up a steep grade (about 4%) to the smeilter and its slag pile. This elevation is achieved through risers made of foam until the mountain rises to meet the elevation, then the track cuts into the side of the mountain.
## 7. MAIN SCENIC FEATURES
* **Water Features:** There are two rivers that dominate the layout, Elk River which comes down the south side of the mountain, beginning at Moose Lake, past Timberline Lumber at Mill Pond, then makes a turn to the west, carving into the landscape to create the major water feature on the layout, Elk River Gorge, before exiting the layout on the west edge of the East leg. The other river, Pine Valley River (placeholder name), comes down from the mountain springs on the west side of the mountain and forms the southern border of the PVJ Yard. It meanders westward off the edge of the layout where the two tracks, the mainline and the mine spur cross over it with curved bridges.
