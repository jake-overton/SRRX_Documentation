### **Edge of Town Buildings (Silver Ridge):** 
* A **large one-story residence** to be placed on the northside of the Elk River overlooking the start of the gorge (where it turns westward). It is a wealthy Denverite's second mountain home that is leased to the director of the Federal Building.
* A very non-descript building on the very south side of the layout on a hill overlooking Silver Ridge. It has a chain link fence with razor wire on top. The parking lot holds two Chevrolet Suburbans, painted black with darkened windows. The building's windows are similarly obscured, and there is a communications satellite on the roof. The building has no markings, save the cryptic logo over the front door. This building screams Secret Federal Building, and has no business being in a remote mountain town. Everyone knows it is there, but nobody asks questions.
* Three Sears mail-order buildings (still need to be painted and electrified) sit on the east side of the town next to the tracks. The buildings have been there for decades and are well maintained, despite their dubious origin and questionable quality. They have become beloved fixtures in Silver Ridge simply because they fought the odds and are still standing.
### Pine Valley Junction
* The plans for this town, which predates but has been superceded by Silver Ridge, are still being developed. Currently, there is a yard office (to be painted), a pub (Green Door Lounge), a bridge over the river from the depot area and the yard, a warehouse and an interchange location for goods transfer. More needs to be put in, and the landscape thought through. This is the working class town, sometimes visited by tourists and railfans, but mostly for railroad workers and the supporting economic infrastructure. It has a bar ready to be assemled, but could use some homes, a general store, perhaps a gas station, etc. The yard also needs some infrastructure buildings, an engine repair facility at minimum. Water or sand, a location for some derelict cars (there is a steam tender in the fleet), the snow plow needs a home as well.
