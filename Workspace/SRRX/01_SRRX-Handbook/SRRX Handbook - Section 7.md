# SECTION 7 – Operating Rules & Procedures

## 7.1. General Operating Philosophy
The Silver Ridge Railway operates as a common carrier serving the communities and industries of the Silver Ridge valley. It is a working railroad — not a showcase — and every movement on the property reflects that purpose. Trains run on time, cars move to revenue, and the railroad earns its place in the valley.

* The Silver Ridge Railway shall be operated as a prototype shortline railroad of the diesel era, 1970s through 2000s. All operations, equipment assignments, and crew conduct shall reflect practices appropriate to that era and territory.
* The railroad exists to serve its customers. Every operating session shall include at least one revenue movement — a mine run, a manifest freight, a local switching assignment, or the Valley Flyer passenger service.
* Realistic operation is the standard. Deliberate, purposeful movements are preferred over speed. Cars shall be spotted, pulled, and classified as they would be on a working shortline — not simply cycled around the loop.
* The Valley Flyer operates on a fixed priority. Freight movements shall yield to scheduled Valley Flyer operations. See Rule 7.6 for full priority statement.
* Operators are responsible for the equipment assigned to them. Locomotives and cars shall be handled with care appropriate to N scale mechanisms. No equipment shall be forced through a turnout, over a grade, or into a clearance point without first confirming the path is set and clear.
* If a derailment occurs, operations shall pause. The cause shall be identified before resuming. Persistent derailments at a specific location shall be noted for track maintenance.

## 7.2. Timetable & Train Orders
The Silver Ridge Railway operates under a simplified timetable authority appropriate to a single-track shortline. Pine Valley Junction serves as the controlling agency for all train movements. In the absence of a dispatcher, the timetable governs.

* The SRRX timetable establishes the scheduled times and priority of regular trains. The Valley Flyer holds first-class timetable authority on all mainline segments.
* Unscheduled freight movements — mine runs, local freights, helper assignments — operate as extras. Extras have no timetable authority and shall not occupy the mainline when a scheduled train is due.
* Pine Valley Junction is the point of origin for all outbound movements. Trains departing PVJ shall have all cars classified, all switches properly set, and the departure track clear before movement begins.
* Train orders may be issued to authorize extra movements, modify scheduled times, or impose speed restrictions. During single-operator sessions, the operator functions as both dispatcher and crew. Train order discipline shall still be observed — a written or spoken order clears a movement; assumption does not.
* A train shall not leave a terminal or passing siding until it has authority to proceed. Authority is either timetable schedule (for first-class trains) or a train order (for extras and modified movements).
* The SRRX operates on a meet-and-pass principle at designated sidings. In the event of a simulated meet, the inferior train shall take the siding and clear the main before the superior train's scheduled arrival time.
* Timetable schedules and designated meet points are defined in the Operating Timetable. [OPERATING TIMETABLE — TO BE DEVELOPED] See Appendix for current schedule.

## 7.3. Mainline Movement Rules
The Silver Ridge Railway mainline forms a continuous loop between Pine Valley Junction and Silver Ridge town, with two distinct routes. Both pass through tunnels under Silver Ridge Mountain — but different ones, at different points in the run.

The River Subdivision (River Run) departs the PVJ yard eastbound, enters the outer tunnel almost immediately, and loops around to emerge heading south along the east leg. From there it passes below Timberline Lumber, follows the Silver Ridge River valley south, and enters Silver Ridge from the east.

The Scenic Subdivision (Scenic Run) departs PVJ westbound, loops around the west end of the north leg, and passes under the mine spur — which has climbed to elevation above it by this point. It reverses direction and enters the team track to service the warehouse and pick up whatever is waiting for it. The consist then heads northeast into the inner tunnel, loops around to exit heading southwest, and continues through the National Park District past Timberline Fuel Depot turnout, stopping to set out or spot cars as required, then continues south across the Silver Ridge Gorge before entering Silver Ridge from the west.

The River Run is the working route — Timberline Lumber, the river valley, a straightforward run into town. The Scenic Run is the scenic route — tunnel loop, National Park, gorge crossing — and it is the designated path of the Valley Flyer. Neither route is operationally preferred for freight. The train order governs.

* All mainline movements shall be authorized before the locomotive leaves the terminal or siding. No train shall enter the mainline on assumption.
* The mainline is single track throughout. Only one train may occupy a mainline block at a time. Meets and passes shall occur at designated sidings only.
* At the start of each operating session, the dispatcher shall assign a route — River Run or Scenic Run — to each scheduled train. Route assignments shall be issued as part of the train order. A train shall not change route mid-run without a new order.
* The River Run serves Timberline Lumber on the east leg. Setouts and pickups for Timberline Lumber shall be made on the River Run unless a specific train order authorizes otherwise.
* The Scenic Run serves the Timberline Fuel Depot turnout and crosses the Silver Ridge Gorge. It is the designated route for the Valley Flyer in all normal operations. The gorge crossing is a restricted speed zone. See Rule 7.5.
* The Silver Ridge Mine is served by a separate spur off the north leg. Mine run movements operate independently of the mainline loop and are governed under Rule 7.7. A mine run shall clear the north leg and be staged at PVJ before a mainline departure is authorized on the same segment.
* Freight trains may operate on either route as assigned. Where a freight movement and the Valley Flyer are both scheduled, the freight shall be routed on the River Run where practical, keeping the two trains on separate segments.
* All mainline movements shall approach turnouts at switching speed before the points. A turnout shall not be taken at track speed unless specifically designated as a high-speed crossover.
* In the event of a simulated delay — equipment failure, schedule conflict, or operational hold — the affected train shall clear the mainline and take the nearest available siding before stopping.
* Designated meet points, passing sidings, and route-specific running times are listed in the Operating Timetable. [TIMETABLE APPENDIX PENDING]

## 7.4. Yard Operations — Pine Valley Junction
Pine Valley Junction yard is not a large facility. It handles what it needs to handle — classification, interchange, engine staging, and departure — with a handful of tracks and whatever power is available. Formal yard hierarchy gives way to practical problem-solving. The goal at the start of every session is simple: the right cars on the right train, pointed the right direction, with a clear path out.

* The 70-Ton GE switcher is the preferred yard power at PVJ. When it is available and operational, road units are kept clear of yard work. When it is not, the road unit does the work — that is the nature of a shortline.
* Track assignments at PVJ are working guidelines, not fixed designations. The Helper Engine Pocket, Team Track, and yard leads reflect normal practice. When space is tight, cars go where they fit and the crew works around it.
* Cars destined for D&RGW interchange shall be identified and spotted as a group before the session ends. Exact track placement depends on available space. The intent is that interchange cars are findable and accessible, not buried behind working freight.
* The Helper Engine Pocket is the preferred staging location for a helper assigned to mine duty. If the pocket is occupied or blocked, the helper may stage on any available clear track at the east end of the yard.
* A train shall be assembled and inspected before departure. All cars in consist order, locomotive coupled, departure route clear. This does not require a formal checklist — it requires that the crew has eyes on the train before it moves.
* Yard limits extend to the east end of the yard lead. Road trains entering yard limits shall reduce speed and give way to any switching movement in progress. When both a road movement and a yard movement need the same track, the road movement waits.
* Engine servicing at PVJ is performed at the fuel and sand facilities adjacent to the yard office. [FACILITY PLACEMENT TO BE CONFIRMED IN SECTION 5] A locomotive assigned to a road run shall be serviced before departure when practical. On a busy session, the crew uses judgment.

## 7.5. Speed Restrictions
The Silver Ridge Railway is not a fast railroad. The territory does not permit it — tunnels, river crossings, steep mine grades, and tight curves through the National Park District all demand that crews run with judgment rather than speed. The restrictions below are standing orders. They apply every session, every movement, without exception.

* Track speed on the mainline is the maximum permitted in open territory with no other restriction in effect. Track speed is not a target — it is a ceiling. Crews are expected to run at a pace appropriate to the consist, the grade, and the conditions.
* Switching speed applies at all times within yard limits, on all industry spurs, at all turnouts, and on the mine spur. A movement at switching speed is a movement under full control — able to stop within half the visible track ahead.
* The Silver Ridge Gorge bridge is a restricted zone. All movements — freight and passenger — shall reduce to switching speed before the bridge approach and maintain switching speed for the full crossing. No exceptions.
* Both tunnels on the north leg shall be entered at reduced speed. A train shall be under control before the tunnel portal and shall not accelerate until clear of the far portal. This applies in both directions on both bores.
* The mine spur grade is the steepest operation on the railroad. Loaded ore movements descending the grade shall be taken at minimum speed. A loaded car shall never be allowed to roll free on the mine grade. Helper engine operations on the mine grade are governed under Rule 7.7.
* The National Park District curves on the Scenic Run require reduced speed throughout the district. The Valley Flyer, despite its passenger status, is not exempt from this restriction — smooth operation through the curves is preferred over schedule recovery speed.
* Approach speed to any turnout, siding, or industry lead shall be reduced before the points. A train shall not arrive at a turnout at track speed and expect to stop in time. The crew sets the approach, not the brake.
* Speed restrictions are in effect regardless of DCC throttle setting or consist weight. When in doubt, slow down. [SPECIFIC SCALE SPEED BENCHMARKS TO BE ESTABLISHED DURING LAYOUT COMMISSIONING — UPDATE THIS SECTION AT THAT TIME]

## 7.6. Valley Flyer Operating Priority
The Valley Flyer is the Silver Ridge Railway's passenger and express service, operating between the Pine Valley Junction and Silver Ridge depots via the Scenic Subdivision (Scenic Run) — through the National Park District, past theTimberline Fuel Depot, and across the gorge. It carries tourists, mail, and light express freight. It is the public face of the railroad in the valley, and it runs on time.

On a shortline where freight pays the bills, giving a passenger train first priority is a statement of character. The Flyer earns its place — the express revenue, the goodwill of the National Park concession, the tourist trade. It does not wait for ore cars.

* The Valley Flyer holds first-class timetable authority on all mainline segments. No freight movement, extra, or mine run shall occupy or block the Flyer's route when the Flyer is within its schedule window.
* The Scenic Run is the Flyer's designated path in all normal operations. A freight train assigned to the Scenic Run shall complete its movement or take a siding before the Flyer's departure window opens. It shall not be in the Flyer's path.
* Occasionally, the Flyer will choose to do the River Run to add variety to the passenger experience. This will not be a regularly scheduled run, but at the discretion of the Dispatch and passenger request.
* At Pine Valley Junction, the Flyer's departure track shall be clear and ready before any other outbound movement is staged. The Flyer loads and departs first. Freight work resumes after it clears the yard limits.
* At Silver Ridge depot, the Flyer has priority access to the station platform. Freight cars shall not occupy the platform track when the Flyer is due. If the platform track is occupied, the freight movement shall be cleared before the Flyer's arrival window.
* In the event of a session where the Valley Flyer is not operated — no consist assembled, no operator available — freight movements may use the Scenic Run freely. The Flyer's priority is only active when the Flyer is running.
* The Valley Flyer consist is: F9A locomotive, passenger coach, observation car. The consist shall be assembled and inspected at PVJ before departure. A Flyer that departs without its full consist is not the Flyer — it is a locomotive movement and holds no special priority.
* Scheduled departure and arrival times for the Valley Flyer are established in the Operating Timetable. [TIMETABLE APPENDIX PENDING] Until the timetable is formalized, the Flyer departs PVJ at the start of the operating session and returns on a reasonable running time as agreed by the crew.

## 7.7. Helper Engine Operations — Silver Ridge Mine
The Silver Ridge Mine spur climbs from the north leg mainline through three switchback legs connected by two turnouts, rising at grades between 2.6% and 3.3%. There is no runaround at the mine. The mine can spot two cars comfortably, three at a push depending on car length. These are the facts that govern helper operations — the rules below follow from them.

* Helper engine assignments are made at Pine Valley Junction. The assigned helper is staged in the Helper Engine Pocket at the east end of the PVJ yard, clear of all mainline and yard leads, until called for service.
* A helper shall not depart the pocket without a train order authorizing the movement. The order shall identify the train to be assisted and confirm the consist size — no more than three cars, typically two.
* Ascending with empties — standard movement: Helper engines assigned to the mine run perform two functions in a single cycle. On the uphill movement, the helper pushes the consist from the helper pocket at PVJ up the mine branch switchbacks to the Silver Ridge Mine. The helper cuts away at the first or second switchback, while empties are spotted and loaded cars are picked up.
* Once cut away, the helper holds on the switchback leg, clear of the turnout it just cleared, and waits. Once released, if there are empty cars at the smelter, the helper first picks them up at the smelter and takes them to the yard at PVJ. It then returns light to the turnout on the mine spur waiting to assist with the loaded cars coming from the mine.
* On the downhill movement with the loaded consist, the helper is not required, the grade works in the train's favor and the road engine provides braking control through the switchbacks. At the base of the switchbacks, at the smelter branch turnout, the helper rejoins the consist on the downhill end. Both engines push the loaded cars up the smelter spur grade to the smelter.
* The helper then returns light to the helper pocket, either coupled to the road engine or running separately, at the crew's discretion. Under no circumstances does the helper attempt the loaded smelter grade alone.
* The helper cut point is the crew's call. With two light empties on a cooperative day, cutting at the first turnout is reasonable. With three cars or difficult conditions, the helper stays coupled through the second turnout before cutting away. The grades are 2.7%, 3.3%, and 2.6% on the three legs respectively — the middle leg is the hardest, and the helper's job is to get the consist through it.
* Descending with loaded ore cars — road engine only: There is no helper on the loaded descent. The road engine climbs empty to the mine, couples to the loaded cars, and works the consist back down through the switchbacks under its own control. The engine pulls the first leg leading into the tunnel, pushes through to the next turnout, then pulls back to the turnout where the helper engine is waiting. Both engines then push the loaded cars to the smelter. Loaded cars on the descent shall be taken at minimum speed at all times. The crew controls the movement. No car rolls free on this grade.
* The mine has no runaround. Once the road engine is coupled to the loaded cars it is committed to the descent movement. All switching at the mine — spotting empties, pulling loads — shall be planned before the engine couples up. There is no room to reconsider once the cars are attached.
* Mine consist size: two cars is the working standard. Three cars is the maximum and requires confirming clear spotting space before the movement begins. A consist of three cars on the 3.3% middle leg is the most demanding movement on the railroad — treat it accordingly.
* NE corner access note: The mine spur switchbacks have limited reach from the aisle. [CONFIRM REACH CLEARANCE DURING LAYOUT CONSTRUCTION] Helper coupling, uncoupling, and car spotting at the mine should be planned as a deliberate, unhurried operation. An uncoupling tool is recommended.
* Helper engine selection: the 70-Ton GE switcher is the standard helper for typical mine consists of two cars. Three-car consists or difficult conditions may warrant a road unit as helper. The crew assigns appropriate power at PVJ before the movement begins.

## 7.8. Radio and Communication
On a shortline like the SRRX, radio is how the railroad talks to itself. The dispatcher at PVJ talks to the road crew on the mainline. The road crew talks to the helper on the mine grade. Nobody is guessing what the other end is doing. In single-operator sessions, radio discipline is a habit of mind rather than a literal requirement — but the language and the discipline are worth maintaining. It keeps the operation feeling like a railroad.

* The SRRX operates on a single assigned radio channel for all train and yard communications. All operators on the layout use the same channel. Side conversations and non-operational chatter shall use a separate channel or be held off radio.
* Assigned operating channel: [TO BE ASSIGNED — COORDINATE WITH LAYOUT ROOM SETUP] All throttles and any radio equipment shall be set to this channel before operations begin.
* Standard SRRX radio calls use the railroad name and train or unit identifier. A road crew calls themselves by train number. The yard calls itself as 'Pine Valley.' The helper identifies as 'Helper.' Example: 'Pine Valley, this is the Flyer, ready to depart.' 'Helper, road engine, ready to couple.'
* All movement authorities — train orders, track clearances, departure releases — shall be confirmed by radio before the movement begins. An authority given and not acknowledged is not an authority in effect.
* On the mine grade, the road engine and helper shall maintain radio contact throughout the ascent. The helper shall confirm cut-away by radio before releasing from the consist. The road engine acknowledges before proceeding to the mine.
* The Valley Flyer crew shall radio PVJ on departure and on arrival at Silver Ridge. This is a schedule confirmation, not a request for authority — the Flyer holds timetable authority. The call keeps the dispatcher informed of the Flyer's position on the layout.
* In single-operator sessions, radio protocol is observed internally — the operator speaks the calls, acknowledges the moves, and maintains the discipline of position reporting even without a second crew. This is not mandatory, but it is strongly encouraged. It builds the operational habits that multi-operator sessions depend on.
* Engine Driver (DCC throttle app) communications are handled via the layout wifi network. [DEDICATED ANDROID DEVICE STILL NEEDED — CURRENT PHONE HAS WIFI/INTERNET CONFLICT] Until resolved, single-throttle operation only.
* In the event of a lost radio contact or throttle dropout during a movement, the train stops and holds position until communication is restored. A train without communication does not proceed.

## 7.9. Fouling Points and Clearance Rules
A fouling point is the location beyond which a car or locomotive occupying one track will interfere with movement on an adjacent track. On a compact N scale layout, fouling points are closer than they feel. The SRRX has limited track space throughout — at PVJ, at the mine, at Silver Ridge depot, at every industry spur. Clearance discipline is not a formality. It is what keeps equipment on the rails and out of each other's way.

* A car or locomotive is clear of a turnout when its full length — including couplers — is beyond the fouling point of that turnout. Trucks and couplers overhang; the body of the car alone is not the measure of clearance.
* Before any turnout is thrown, the operator shall visually confirm that all equipment on both routes is clear of the fouling point. A turnout is never thrown against an occupied track without first confirming clearance.
* At Pine Valley Junction, the yard leads and departure tracks are compact. A car spotted on an adjacent track may foul the lead even when it appears clear. When in doubt at PVJ, walk the track before moving the switch. Eyes on the equipment, not the throttle.
* Industry spurs throughout the railroad — Timberline Lumber, Timberline Fuel Depot, Silver Ridge depot, the mine — are short by design. Spotting a car on a spur shall include confirming that the car clears the mainline or lead it was pulled from. A spotted car that fouls the main is not spotted — it is a problem.
* The Silver Ridge Gorge bridge has no room for error on approach or departure. Equipment shall be fully clear of the bridge approach turnout before any switch is thrown at that location. The gorge crossing is treated as a restricted clearance zone.
* Fouling points at PVJ and all industry locations shall be physically marked on the layout during construction. [FOULING POINT MARKERS TO BE INSTALLED DURING TRACK LAYING — CONFIRM LOCATIONS AGAINST FINAL TRACK PLAN] Until marked, operators shall apply a conservative estimate and verify visually.
* On the mine spur switchbacks, each leg is a clearance point for the leg above and below it. A consist reversing through a turnout shall be confirmed clear of the previous leg before the turnout is thrown for the next movement. There is no room on the mine grade to recover from a fouling error.
* In multi-operator sessions, no operator shall throw a turnout on another operator's active segment without first communicating the intent and receiving acknowledgment. Turnout authority follows the train — the crew working a segment controls its switches.
* A derailment at or near a turnout shall be treated as a potential fouling event. Operations on adjacent tracks shall pause until the derailed equipment is rerailed and confirmed clear of all fouling points before resuming.
